# Evidence Rule

Use this rule for claims that affect an interpretation, conclusion, or recommendation. It answers two questions in order: **does evidence exist**, and **is it good enough for this use**?

## Evidence classes

- **User context:** what the user states about their own situation. Treat it as the working current-state anchor unless it is ambiguous. Do not demand external corroboration.
- **External source:** records, reporting, data, statements, analysis, or other material found by the agent. Inspect and cite it.
- **Supplied artifact:** an article, post, screenshot, transcript, or link the user asks the agent to assess. Scrutinize it like an external source; supplying it does not make its claims true.
- **Assessment:** the agent's synthesis, inference, forecast, or judgment. Label it and show its factual basis.

## Credibility and fit

A credible source has traceable origin, relevant access and competence, inspectable methods or basis, accountability, and visible limits or incentives.

Good evidence is credible evidence that also fits the exact claim, population, place, date, mechanism, and decision. It is sufficiently direct, rigorous, complete, current, precise, and independently supported for the cost of being wrong.

Evidence that an announcement occurred does not by itself prove legal effect, implementation, broad scope, causation, effectiveness, or personal impact.

## Claim states

Keep these states distinct:

- **Established:** direct evidence or independent sources support the narrow claim.
- **Partial:** relevant evidence exists, but scope, precision, implementation, or support is incomplete.
- **Contradicted:** inspected evidence materially conflicts with the claim.
- **No evidence:** required evidence could not be found or inspected. This is not weak evidence.

A true detail used to imply more than it supports may be **accurate but misleadingly framed**; state both parts.

## Corroboration gate

An external or supplied-artifact claim that affects a recommendation and lacks independent support is **single-source**. It cannot be the sole basis for costly, urgent, or irreversible action.

Independence means the sources do not merely repeat the same report, press release, anonymous claim, dataset, or owner-controlled account.

## Due diligence

For each important conclusion, be able to explain:

- why it matters to the request;
- what evidence standard applies;
- what direct evidence, independent support, exceptions, and counterevidence were checked;
- why the evidence is enough or not enough;
- why further checking is unlikely to change the result enough to justify its cost or delay; and
- what would reopen the conclusion.

Run a sensitivity check: remove or discount the strongest external source. If the result changes, disclose the dependency and lower the strength of the conclusion or action.

## Failure and blind spots

A snippet, inaccessible page, repeated source chain, source reputation, political rating, or assessment cannot substitute for inspected evidence.

Put a limitation on the finding it affects:

`Unverified: could not inspect <needed evidence>, because <reason>.`

When there is no adequate evidence, defer the dependent conclusion and name a concrete reopening event, source, measurement, deadline, or user fact.
