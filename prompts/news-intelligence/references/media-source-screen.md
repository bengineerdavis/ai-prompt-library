# Media Source Screen

Use only for media sources that materially affect the answer. Do not turn every digest into an outlet audit.

## Check the source

- **Reporting practice:** Does it identify original evidence, distinguish firsthand from secondhand knowledge, and separate reporting, analysis, opinion, advocacy, prediction, and advertising?
- **Corrections and transparency:** Are author, date, ownership, funding, sponsorship, conflicts, and corrections visible where relevant?
- **Framing:** Does the headline match the body? Do certainty, causality, urgency, scale, and emotional language fit the evidence? What denominator, comparison, affected group, exception, or counterview is missing?
- **Orientation and incentives:** Consider political leaning, ideology, owner and sponsor interests, audience expectations, access dependence, commercial goals, reputation, and engagement incentives. State only motivations that are supported or clearly labeled as possible incentives.
- **Corroboration:** Trace the claim to its origin. Several outlets repeating one release or unnamed report are one source chain, not independent confirmation.

## Apply the screen

Political orientation and third-party bias ratings show where framing or selection may deserve more scrutiny. They do not prove a claim true or false, and “center” does not mean accurate.

Prefer claim-level checking over outlet-level scoring. Use services such as NewsGuard, Ad Fontes, or AllSides as methodology or triage inputs when available, not as final arbiters.

Report source context only when it changes interpretation or confidence. A compact form is enough:

- **Relevant source context:** <orientation, ownership, incentive, or practice>
- **What it could affect:** <selection, framing, omission, wording, or confidence>
- **What was checked:** <direct record, correction, counterview, or independent source>
